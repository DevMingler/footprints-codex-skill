# Report Design v1

## Goal

Produce a reflective account of what emerged across a person's ChatGPT
history. The report should feel recognisable and useful without judging,
profiling, or reducing the person to activity counts.

## Analysis Workflow

1. Reconstruct the selected path of every conversation.
2. Build a user-first evidence inventory across the full date range.
3. Identify candidate projects, themes, questions, shifts, and unfinished ideas.
4. Test each candidate against evidence from different conversations and dates.
5. Check for recency bias, repetition, assistant contamination, and overclaiming.
6. Write the report only after reviewing the full period.

Frequency is one signal, not the definition of importance. Specificity,
duration, follow-through, emotional or creative significance, and later
reappearances can make a less frequent thread meaningful.

## Evidence Standard

A finding is strongest when it is:

* grounded in the user's own messages;
* supported across multiple conversations or points in time;
* specific enough to distinguish from a generic observation;
* consistent with actions, artefacts, decisions, or repeated questions.

Assistant responses may clarify context but cannot establish a claim about the
user by themselves.

When evidence is limited, the report should use tentative language or omit the
finding.

## Report Sections

### The Shape of the Period

A short opening synthesis of the dominant movement across the history.

### Major Projects and Initiatives

The clearest bodies of sustained work, including their development or changing
role over time.

### Recurring Themes and Persistent Questions

Ideas, concerns, interests, or questions that returned in different contexts.

### Seasons and Shifts

A light chronology of notable changes in attention, approach, or emphasis.

### Threads Worth Revisiting

Promising, meaningful, or unfinished ideas that may have faded from view.

### Closing Reflection

A restrained synthesis that connects the findings without turning them into a
score, diagnosis, or fixed identity.

Sections should be omitted when the export does not support them.

## Presentation

* Aim for 1,200 to 1,800 words.
* Prefer specific observations over exhaustive lists.
* Use month-and-year evidence anchors for important findings.
* Paraphrase conversation content rather than reproducing private passages.
* Distinguish direct evidence from interpretation.
* Avoid generic praise, productivity language, and personality claims.
